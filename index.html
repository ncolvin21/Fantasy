The Delta Board

Consensus projections tell you what a player did. This board scores what produces the points — workhorse share, first-read targets, red-zone touches, yards per route run, designed runs, pace, play-caller, and the betting market's read on every offense — then measures that against ESPN’s own draft data — the board you will actually be picking from. The distance is the delta.

What the signal is made of

Weights differ by position because the things that predict points differ by position. Each input is scored against the rest of the position, not against an absolute.

Rookies are scored on a different recipe — they have no NFL usage to measure, so draft capital sets the expectation and college production and landing spot adjust it. Their rows carry an R; click one to see the inputs. When a metric is missing for a player, its weight is redistributed across the ones that exist rather than filled with a guess — and the row is marked with a lower confidence dot. The composite is capped at ±18% of the consensus projection, so the signal re-ranks players without pretending to replace the projection.

Where replacement level sits

Every value on this page is measured against these four numbers. Change the league and they move — which is why one ranking list cannot serve both drafts.

The betting layer

Season-long implied points per game for all 32 offenses, derived from every game's spread and total. This is the sharpest available forecast of scoring environment.

ESPN defence ADP — League B only

ESPN forces a D/ST slot and only 32 exist across the league, so the pool drains far earlier than on a consensus board: four are gone by pick 110, eight by 155. Do not plan on streaming defence late in League B.

The exploitable split. Win total and scoring environment come apart badly for two teams. Dallas carries only a 9.5-win total but grades as the league's best fantasy environment — 15 of its 17 games have a total of 48 or higher. Chicago is the same phenomenon one tier down, with the easiest schedule in the league. Running the other way, fantasy drafters are spending premium capital on players from the five offenses Vegas rates worst — Las Vegas, Miami and Arizona above all.

            What could not be sourced

            Two of the metrics you asked for are behind subscriptions or render client-side. Here is exactly what is missing and what stands in for it.

              How the board is built

              Five layers, in this order. The first two move slowly; layers three and four are what you re-run.

              LAYER 01

              A price, from ESPN

              ESPN’s own averageDraftPosition field, not a cross-site consensus — because a consensus board prices a draft you are not in. Spot-checked against ESPN’s live API and exact to the decimal.

              ESPN’s default league is 10 teams by 17 rounds, so the board ends at 170 picks. Players past that are marked FA: on a consensus board they have an ADP, in your league they are free.

              LAYER 02

              A points baseline

              FantasyPros consensus and CBS season projections, blended after normalising CBS onto the FantasyPros scale. This layer exists because value over replacement needs points to subtract — it is the substrate, not the opinion.

              LAYER 03

              Availability, in two parts

              News is this week’s reporting: a torn ACL is a 0.03, a hamstring that costs three weeks is a 0.97. It decays in days.

              Durability is the chronic half, and it had to be split out because consensus projections hand every player seventeen games. It is expected games over seventeen, from documented 2025 games played, age, and surgery inside the last year. It is applied at every position so it is not a thumb on the scale against one of them.

              LAYER 04

              The signal

              2025 opportunity and efficiency, weighted per the recipe at the top of this page, plus the betting market's implied points per game for the player's offense. Everything here is sourced — Pro-Football-Reference for usage and red-zone splits, PFF and StickToTheModel for yards per route run, Sharp Football for pace, RotoWire and CBS for play-caller history, and derived implied totals off all 272 games.

              Sources disagree more than they admit. PFF and StickToTheModel differ by 84 routes on Puka Nacua alone, so figures are only averaged inside a matching season window — never across two.

              LAYER 05

              Your league, not a generic one

              Points become value over replacement using the actual starter demand in each league. League A starts 42 wide receivers, so WR42 is free. League B starts 24, and only twelve tight ends — which is why the same tight end is a fringe pick in one draft and a top-fifteen asset in the other.

              IN SEASON

              What changes weekly

              The signal rebuilds on this year's usage rather than last year's from about Week 4, when target share and snap share stabilise. Before that the 2025 baseline is still the better predictor.

              The betting layer moves every week: game totals and spreads become implied team totals, which is the single best weekly input there is. ESPN's public scoreboard feed carries DraftKings lines with no key and no rate limit — queried per date, not per week, which is where it truncates.

              WhenWhat gets re-run, and why

              Night beforeFull refresh: ADP has moved for two more weeks of mocks, and late scratches land. This is the version you draft from.

              Reading the delta. A green +18 means this model ranks him eighteen slots higher than the room does — you can wait, and he should still be there. A red −30 does not mean the player is bad; it means at that price you are paying for production the signal does not see. Deltas inside ±8 are noise.

              Reading the signal. 50 is positional average. It is a relative score, not a projection: a 94 on a 30-year-old back means his 2025 usage was extraordinary, not that he will repeat it. Age, injury history and regression live in the news layer and in the notes, not in the signal.

              Known soft spots. ESPN publishes ADP for its 10-team default, which maps exactly onto League A and is an approximation for League B — a 12-team ESPN league runs 34 picks deeper, so treat the late-round deltas there as directional. League B’s flex is modelled as roughly 55% receiver, 40% back and 5% tight end, which is the usual full-PPR split at these starter counts; if your league flexes backs more often than that, running back value rises a little and receiver value falls. Kickers are absent from the feed entirely, and defences are listed but not projected. A rookie’s score is measured against the rest of his draft class, not against the position — 50 there means an average 2026 rookie, not an average NFL player. And League B is modelled with no flex, exactly as the settings were given; if that league does have a flex spot, receiver value rises materially.
